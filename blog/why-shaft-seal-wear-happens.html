Why Shaft Seal Wear Happens (and How to Spot It)

Shaft seal wear is one of the most common maintenance problems in rotating equipment. It happens in predictable ways. ## The usual causes Contamination is the biggest culprit. Dust, grit, sand and debris get behind the seal lip and grind a groove into the shaft. Heat degrades the seal and the surface. Misalignment and vibration put uneven load on the contact area. ## How the wear looks A worn shaft seal surface usually shows a score line or groove right where the seal lip contacts. It may look polished, grooved, or pitted. Once it's there, the seal can't hold. ## Why it matters A leaking seal lets lubricant out and contamination in. Left unchecked, that cascade leads to bearing failure, seal damage, and eventually catastrophic equipment failure. It usually starts at the seal contact area. ## The fix Once the surface is worn, a repair sleeve is the fast fix. It restores the shaft to a fresh counterface using the same seal size. In real-world testing, DuraSleeve lasted at least 2x longer than a legacy sleeve. **The bottom line:** seal surface wear is a groove in the most-exposed part of your shaft. Catch it early and a repair sleeve fixes it before it becomes a much bigger problem.
