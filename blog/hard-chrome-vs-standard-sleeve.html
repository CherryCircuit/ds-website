Hard Chrome vs a Standard Sleeve Surface: Why It Matters

Not all repair sleeves are the same. The surface that contacts the seal is the part that does the work, and that's where DuraSleeve differs. ## What the seal needs A shaft seal seats against a smooth, wear-resistant counterface. If that surface is soft, it wears quickly, the seal leaks, and you're back to repairing the shaft. ## The hard chrome difference DuraSleeve uses a hard chrome surface around 68 Rockwell C. That's a much harder surface than a standard sleeve. Hardness supports wear resistance — the surface resists the abrasion and contaminant embedding that cause grooves and leaks. ## Low friction and corrosion too The hard chrome surface also holds lubricant, reducing friction and heat at the sealing lip. And it sits over an SAE/ANSI 304 stainless steel base, giving strong corrosion resistance. ## The result in practice In DuraSleeve's own real-world testing against a legacy sleeve, DuraSleeve lasted at least 2x longer. The harder surface is the reason the repair lasts. **The bottom line:** if you're choosing a repair sleeve, look at the surface. A hard chrome surface resists wear, reduces friction, and resists corrosion — that's what makes the repair last.
