Shaft Repair vs Shaft Replacement: What It Really Costs

When a shaft seal surface wears out, you have two paths: repair the surface, or replace the shaft. The sticker price of a sleeve is small, but the real comparison is the total cost of downtime. ## The cost of a rebuild A shaft replacement or rebuild isn't just the part. Add in labor, machining, and the production lost while the equipment is down. In many cases the downtime alone costs far more than the part. ## The sleeve option A shaft repair sleeve is a thin-walled sleeve that installs over the worn surface. Because it uses the same seal size, there's no machining and no engineering change. The repair happens in minutes, not days. ## Why a harder surface pays off If a sleeve wears out again quickly, you pay the downtime cost again. DuraSleeve's hard chrome surface is around 68 Rockwell C, much harder than a standard sleeve surface. In DuraSleeve's own real-world testing against a legacy sleeve, DuraSleeve lasted at least 2x longer. ## The real comparison A repair sleeve costs a fraction of a shaft replacement and gets you running fast. When it lasts at least 2x longer, the lifetime cost drops sharply. **The bottom line:** compare total cost, not sticker price. A shaft repair sleeve that lasts at least 2x longer is usually far cheaper than a replacement once downtime is counted.
